Aave: the exit ramp, and what works now

DeFi Aave

Aave's contracts were not exploited. The attacker used it as a currency converter.

1 · Deposit$60M of stolen USDT goes in as collateral, at the moment Tether was racing to freeze it.
20:58–21:01 UTC
2 · Borrow19,762 WETH (about $44.6M) borrowed against it. ETH can't be frozen like USDT.
LlamaRisk
3 · FreezeFirst market freeze lands about 40 minutes later. The attacker never touched Aave again.
21:42 UTC onward
  • The position still stands: $59.9M collateral vs $43.8M debt at LlamaRisk's snapshot, subject to normal liquidation.
  • Aave froze everything, not just stolen tokens, because freezing single reserves would leave other doors open.
  • Not April's KelpDAO exploit: that one left Aave with bad debt. This one has not created bad debt so far.

If you're on Aave: freeze is not a full pause

Blocked

  • New deposits, including collateral top-ups
  • New borrowing
  • Moves that need to supply a frozen asset, like some collateral swaps

Still open

  • Repaying debt
  • Withdrawing, subject to pool liquidity
  • Liquidations, which run as normal

Health factor below 1 means liquidation, and you can't top up while markets stay frozen. Repaying debt is the lever you still have: big withdrawals push borrow rates up, repayments pull them down. Avalanche is on the frozen list with Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base and more. No unfreeze date, so watch Aave's forum and X. Education only, not financial advice.

Robotics Reserve: tokenized options

New project Early

Fully collateralized covered calls on Robinhood Chain, starting with RBTX, tokenized NVDA and SPCX. Contracts are built. The product is not live. Everything here is the project's own description, not an endorsement.

1 · DepositA seller locks tokens in escrow and sets the premium, exercise price and expiration date.
2 · BuyA buyer pays the premium for the right, not the obligation, to buy those tokens at the exercise price before expiry.
3 · SettleIf exercised, the buyer pays ETH and gets the tokens. If not, the tokens return to the seller onchain.
  • Priced in ETH: premiums and exercise payments are both in ETH.
  • Fee split: platform takes 10% of the premium, the seller keeps 90%.
  • Keep it simple: manual exercise only, no reselling of option positions.
  • Tokens, not shares: settlement delivers tokens, not company shares.

Still required before launch

  • An independent security audit
  • A legal assessment
  • Real token compatibility checks

What I couldn't verify

  • The project site wouldn't open for me
  • No independent coverage found
  • No team or auditor named in the description
  • RBTX is also an iShares robotics ETF ticker

Plain-English risk: a covered call pays the seller up front and gives up the upside above the exercise price; the buyer risks the premium. New risks here: smart-contract bugs in the escrow, and thin liquidity in new tokenized assets. Not financial advice.

What we don't know yet, and takeaways

Wrap-up

Watch this weekend

  • Root cause. EIP-7702 delegation, signing infrastructure, or both?
  • The final number. $6.3B from Binance, $6.36B from TRM, ~$3B frozen and rising.
  • When Aave unfreezes, reserve by reserve, once funds stop moving.
  • Withdrawals. Binance expects to resume within 48 hours.
  • The freeze debate on "decentralized" chains.
  • Attribution. TRM and Chainalysis say North Korea; no official confirmation yet.

Takeaways for the community

  • Hot wallets are concentration risk. One signing path controlled billions.
  • Stablecoins are permissioned money. Tether and Circle froze about $2.4B in hours: it protected victims and shows an issuer can say no to any address.
  • BTC and ETH on their own chains can't be frozen.
  • Know your DeFi positions before the storm. Check health factors now, not during a crash.
